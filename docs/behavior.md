## Selection and picking

- Left to right box select encloses; right to left touches. With no filter (the Box selects setting on All, nothing held) the box takes faces, edges and vertices together; holding F, E or V during a box drag restricts it to faces, edges or vertices.
- Shift+click and Ctrl+click both add to or remove from the selection, in the 3D view and in sketches. A plain click in a sketch adds to the selection.
- Double-click on an edge selects the tangent-continuous chain; Ctrl+double-click adds it to the whole selection and keeps everything already selected. The chain continues only where the next edge leaves in the opposite direction (a fold back on itself is not followed). A double-click on a face selects its whole body.
- Pick ranges are 5 px for edges and 8 px for points and come from settings, never inline constants.
- Relationship chips (coplanar, equal radius and so on) are off by default, with All/None and Parallel/Symmetric. Resize hole also resizes same-size holes.
- See-through is the X key; repeated click or Alt+click cycles faces behind.
- Selecting a body in the 3D view scrolls its row into view in the bodies list.
- A global axis can be clicked in the view when no vertex, edge or nearer face is under the pointer, and joined to a face with Ctrl+click; Revolve face then turns the face about it. Revolve face needs only a face; the axis can be chosen in its form. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- In measure mode, a double-click on a candidate acts as a pick and starts no other tool. (decided, 2026-10-26)

## Navigation and view

- Wheel zooms toward the cursor, right-drag orbits, middle-drag pans, including during a tool drag.
- View keys: 0 iso, 1 front, 2 top, 3 right, 4 rear, 5 bottom, 6 left, P projection, V look at face.
- Entering a sketch does not move the camera; the Look at plane button does. It turns the view square to the sketch plane in orthographic projection; when the view was perspective, turning the camera away from the sketch (not zooming or panning) goes back to perspective.

## Keys

- Bare keys may change view, display, arm a tool or change selection. Anything that changes the model or a setting at once needs Ctrl or Alt.
- Every hotkey is user-redefinable except the fixed sketch keys (Ctrl+Z, Escape, Enter, Delete/Backspace). Any mix of Ctrl, Alt and Shift is allowed.
- Measure pick is the rebindable command "measure", default M.
- The page warns when leaving with undo steps or an open sketch (confirmed by the owner, 2026-10-26: also when the sketch holds only geometry and nothing else changed).

## Tools and drags

- A hole whose round wall is split into several faces behaves as one hole: pressing and dragging any part of the wall pushes or pulls the whole hole's radius, and Move hole, Resize hole and Edit hole work on it.
- The display precision setting (1 to 8 decimals, default 3) sets the decimals shown in measurements, sketch dimensions, tool forms, handle labels and drag labels, and how finely mouse drags round; zeros after the last digit are never shown.
- Pressing a hole's center dot selects that hole and shows only the dot as selected (the wall is not tinted); every tool treats it as the hole, as when its wall is selected.
- The hole forms have a Sizes list beside the diameter (and counterbore diameter) field: metric clearance and tap drill sizes M2 to M20, counterbore diameters for cap screws; choosing one fills the field and refreshes the preview. In the Hole Editor card the same sizes, with inch sizes and more classes, come from its Fastener presets list above the fields instead.
- Edit hole shows a grip on the shoulder of a counterbore or countersink (and at the far end of a through hole's step); dragging it sets the step's depth, and a countersink keeps its angle so its mouth widens.
- Edit cut works on a square, slot or other straight-walled cut (through, or ending at a flat floor) selected by a wall or its floor: Move arrows, a depth grip (0 or the body's thickness is through; less leaves a flat floor) and a resize grip that grows every wall by the same distance (negative shrinks).
- Edit cut also has Length and Width resize grips (and fields): each moves only the two ends of the cut along its longer or its shorter side, by the value, for a cut that runs along a world axis; the all-round resize grip is unchanged.
- Pressing on a face or edge selects it and starts its drag in one motion; a press without movement is a click.
- One drag sets one value. Typing digits during a drag starts value entry: Tab switches fields, Enter commits, Escape cancels. The shape moves only on Enter.
- A failed preview keeps the last good size and shows the status in red; release commits the last good size.
- Each tool with a drag parameter has on-screen handles, one per parameter; with a form open a handle sets the form field.
- Handles show their name and value only for the one under the pointer. Diameter parameters have a radius handle plus a perpendicular double-ended diameter handle.
- Holding Shift while left-dragging a face makes push/pull symmetric: a sketch face about its sketch plane, two parallel faces about their mid-plane, one face about the global origin plane or a datum plane. Pressing or releasing Shift during the drag switches the mode from then on (provisional).
- When the push/pull arrow handle on a selected face is dragged, it starts the same drag as pressing the face: measure pick (M; right-click is the view's own menu and right-drag orbits), typed value, Shift symmetric and companion faces all work the same.
- Push/pull in Auto mode: a drag along the face's arrow adds (fuses with every body the extrusion meets) and a drag against it subtracts (cuts every body it meets, also where it passes over empty space); a sketch region uses its own normal, so a region on a body face needs no coplanar test. A subtract that removes nothing changes nothing and the status line says "Nothing to subtract". Symmetric: the arrow side adds and the opposite side subtracts. (owner, 2026-10-28)
- The Push/Pull card opens on every push/pull drag and when the Push/Pull button is clicked, under the nav cube group. Mode buttons Auto, Add, Subtract and New body highlight the current one like the Pick plane button; choosing one leaves Auto. Flip swaps which drag direction adds and which subtracts (no face or sketch normal changes). Symmetric, the two tilt values and the pick-direction vector are fields linked to the gizmo. (owner, 2026-10-28; provisional until hand-checked)
- Holding Alt at any time during a push/pull drag makes the result a New body (always additive, never cuts or merges, whatever the direction or collisions); releasing Alt restores the previous mode; the state at release decides. On a body face, pulling makes the added volume a new body and pushing makes the removed slab a new body while the original stays whole. Shift (symmetric) still works with it. (owner, 2026-10-28; provisional until hand-checked)
- When push/pull is given faces that face opposite ways (a body face with a sketch region), every face is treated as having the normal of the first selected face; the arrow and the ghost use that normal too. (owner, 2026-10-29)
- The push/pull gizmo has a red arrow opposite the face-normal arrow marking the subtract direction; it can be grabbed like the normal arrow, and Flip swaps the two roles. (owner, 2026-10-29: "looks great"; grabbing was added after he could not grab it)
- While a subtracting tool previews (Push/Pull, Helical sweep and Revolve face in Subtract mode, Sweep face, the hole tools, Emboss/Cut along surface), the part of its cutting volume that lies outside every body is drawn as a translucent red ghost; nothing is drawn for additive modes, through holes or slots, or on commit. A preview that removes nothing keeps the model and says why in the status line; Apply refuses. (owner, 2026-10-29: "Push ghost looks awesome")
- The Sketch card has a Flip button: it reverses the sketch plane's normal, every drawn point keeps its place in space, the view turns to look from the new side, and it is one sketch undo step. (owner, 2026-10-28; provisional until hand-checked)
- While a point is being picked (Pick base point, destination point and the other point picks), every gizmo is hidden so none is in the way, and the status line adds "Gizmos hidden during pick action."; the gizmos come back when the pick ends or is cancelled. (owner, 2026-10-28)
- Move and rotate are one tool (Move/Rotate). Rotate and scale pivot about each the current move base point.
- Move/Rotate with edges selected (provisional): the gizmo sits at the edge middle with two arrows square to the edge (none along the edge) and one 120 degree arc between them. Dragging the arc (or typing degrees) turns the arrows about the edge and snaps them square to the global axes and to each adjacent flat face's normal. The edge then moves along the arrows and its neighboring faces follow; a corner slides along the edge to stay on any face that does not move, so slanted edges move within their faces. The arrows swing live as the arc is dragged. Tick marks show the snap angles under the arc; when the gizmo sits square to a face (or the global axes) the arc turns green and its label says so. The snap angles are read from the faces beside the edge as they are after the move so far, so they change every time the edge moves. Turning the arc only turns the arrows; it never moves the edge. In the form, Move X, Y and Z are the edge's world move, Rotate X is the arc, Rotate Y and Z are unused.
- Primitives are separate bodies by default. Sketch extrude are Join if intersecting a body or separate bodies if no intersection with any body.
- When a tool card is open, a click on empty space applies the tool to the selection it was opened on (the click itself is not carried over). Clicking another item, or using any gizmo or handle of the tool (arrows, arcs, grips, labels, typed entry), never applies or closes it: the card stays open on its own selection and the other item is not picked. An array form follows the same rule: a click on empty space applies the array (it no longer asks); picking another tool while an array is open still asks to apply or discard. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- Dimensions are edited in place by double-clicking the text; an edit runs the ordinary tool as one undo step. Impossible edits show the reason in red and change nothing.
- Warnings show in orange; red is for errors.
- Dimension values, leaders and arrowheads are off-white, and they and the handle grips sit on a thin, 60% opaque dark outline (approved), so they stay readable over light bodies without looking hard-edged.
- When Inset face edges offsets overlap (a hole band meets the outer band or another hole band), the outlines merge into one combined outline: the inset face is the face minus every band, cut-off islands stay separate faces, and a distance that covers the whole face reports "The inset is larger than the face".
- Every tool in the all tools drawer can be chosen at any time and works with a correct preselection or a selection made after choosing it: tools that do not fit the current selection are only dimmed (never blocked), choosing one says in the status line what to select, and it starts as soon as the selection fits. (owner, 2026-10-29)
- The command search (opened with / or by clicking its box; Ctrl+K does nothing) lists every tool matching the typed text whatever the selection: a tool whose selection does not fit is only dimmed and sorted after equal matches that fit, and choosing it waits for a selection like the drawer does. (owner, 2026-10-29)

## Bodies

- The bodies list is a persistent plain list on the left of the 3D view, outside the drawer. Rows have a name, eye and isolate; right-click gives Rename, Duplicate, Delete and Edit primitive.
- Clicking a row selects the whole body; tools then use bodies directly.
- Delete (the key) removes the selected bodies (Shift or Ctrl selection in the list) as one undo step; right-click Delete on one of several selected rows removes them all. A clicked sketch or datum plane row is removed by Delete or its right-click Delete too. The key is ignored while typing, in a sketch or with a tool form open.
- The linear array's corner grip is a cube: dragged in the plane of the column and row steps it sets columns and rows independently, one whole step each; while the grip is held, typing digits enters the columns, Tab moves to the rows, Enter commits (whole numbers, at least 1), Escape goes back to the mouse and then cancels; the label shows what is typed.
- Add prism is in the Solids group after Add torus: a regular polygon (6 sides by default) extruded up, with the same form, placement and later editing as Add cone (a bottom and a top diameter, each with radius and diameter handles; a top of 0 makes a true pyramid, a bottom of 0 an upside-down one, both 0 is refused) plus a sides grip.
- Push/pull direction: the default is the face normal, pulled with the arrowhead grabber. The arrow is long: its stem runs through the pick icon and both tilt arcs and ends as far past them as the arrow used to end off the face, so the arrowhead stays grabbable; the pick icon (the target) sits exactly where the two arcs cross, on the arrow's line. It is the UI's pick icon (the same crosshair icon as the sketch's pick buttons): press it, then click a straight edge (or a face, for its normal) to run the arrow and the face drag along that line, turned to point out of the face; clicking the selected face itself goes back to the normal. Two colored arcs are drawn as if on a hemisphere around the arrow, a little larger than the arrow: a red one that swings the arrow toward the x axis of the sketch plane on the face (about its y axis) and a green one toward its y axis (about its x axis). Each is 120 degrees, 60 either side of the arrow's line, so together they give the four cardinal tilts; dragging an arc leans the arrow along it, never closer than 0.1 cosine to the face plane; while an arc is held, typing digits enters the tilt in degrees from the face normal (the sign follows the way the arc was pulled, positive continues that way and negative goes the opposite; typed degrees on one arc keep the other arc's angle, and zero on one arc leaves the other, 90 is cut back to stay off the face plane), Enter applies it, Escape goes back to the mouse and then cancels, and the label shows the typed value (the push/pull distance is typed in its own drag, there is no Tab between the two). The pick icon has no backing or padding, only its own strokes. After pressing it, a click on an edge sets the direction and keeps the face selected and Push/Pull active; a click that is not a straight edge or a face keeps the picker armed. The angle off the face normal is shown only while the direction is not the normal. The choice is forgotten when the selection changes. A selected wall shows no draft angle tag (the Draft tool does drafting).
- Each body has a stable id and an auto name; rename is an undo step.
- When a Boolean union joins bodies that do not touch, the result is one compound body (one row in the list holding several solids); its faces stay pickable, push/pull and the other tools work on it, and it exports as several solids. Moving, rotating, filleting, holes, mirror and the other tools keep it one body even when the model has other bodies; Measure on it shows the number of solids and each solid's volume.
- When a different model is opened (demo, import, project, resume), every body starts visible, solid and out of focus.
- Import, Open and Combine read the file type from the file name: .step, .stp, .stpZ (gzip-compressed STEP), .iges, .igs, .brep, .brp, .3dm (surfaces and solids; meshes and curves in the file are skipped) and .ifc (each product with a body becomes a part named after it, a solid where its triangles close; openings and spaces are skipped and the import says how many). Export STEP keeps writing STEP; the Export CAD list writes .stp, .stpZ, .igs, .brep, .3dm (triangle meshes, so lossy) and .ifc (one building element proxy per body, named after it, with a faceted B-rep in millimeters, so lossy). .ifc needs the optional IfcOpenShell package; without it the app says so and changes nothing. A file of any other type, or one that cannot be read, shows a message and changes nothing.

## Sketch

- Sketches are kept as separate objects. Finishing a sketch adds no faces to the model; closed regions show as tinted view-only profile faces.
- Automatic constraints are on by default (Snaps and shapes; owner, 2026-10-25, replacing the earlier off default); a saved choice in the sketch settings still wins, and the setting Off leaves geometry floating freely. The grid is off by default; grid options live in the gear on the sketch card, not the global settings.
- Sketch card sits under the nav cube: title and gear on the first row, icon row, then Finish and Discard.
- Right-click in a sketch not on sketch geometry ends the current drawing tool.
- Project model edges is a pick mode: pick faces and edges, Enter or the button projects, Escape cancels. Projected edges are snapshots. Projected edges come from the solid's real edge curves: lines stay lines, circles and arcs square to the plane stay true circles and arcs, a circle or ellipse edge seen at an angle becomes a true ellipse; a tilted arc or a part of an ellipse edge becomes an elliptical arc; only a B-spline or other curved edge is a spline through sample points. (owner, 2026-10-25; elliptical arcs ellarc agent, 2026-10-26)
- A sketch dimension's value is a plain number; no expressions or named values yet.
- Clicking a kept sketch's tinted region adds it as a temporary face; clicking away without using it undoes the pick; pulling it into a solid is one undo step together with the pick.
- Closed areas in a sketch are faces even when the geometry that encloses them shares no end points: lines that end on another line split it, so a rectangle with a divider drawn between two of its sides is two faces, and three lines attached to a side enclose a third. Lines that enclose nothing make no face.
- While a sketch shape is being drawn, typing digits starts a size entry for the next point: Tab (or space) moves to the next number, Enter places the shape. Rectangle takes width and height, slot length and width, ellipse major and minor width (full widths), arc radius and sweep degrees; line, circle and polygons take one number.
- Every sketch drawing tool except Line returns to Select once its first shape is complete; Line keeps drawing.
- While a sketch is open, the nav cube's zoom to fit everything fits all geometry of the sketch being edited, and zoom to fit selection fits the selected sketch points and curves. The fit has no minimum size: a sketch a millimeter across fills the view like a large one.
- In a sketch, Delete with only points selected removes what holds them to other geometry (a coincident constraint, or the shared end of two lines, which is split in two) and leaves the lines; a point nothing holds is deleted with its geometry as before.
- The sketch constraint list shows every constraint when nothing is selected, and only those that name the selected curves or points (or points of the selected curves) otherwise. A point where drawn curves meet is listed as an automatic Coincident row; its trash can button splits the point so the curves have their own ends.
- The value of every dimension, in a sketch and in the 3D view, has a small padded gap in the dimension's own extension, leader and arrow lines; nothing else (geometry, faces, grid, model) is hidden behind it, and the text keeps its outline.
- Dragging a single sketch point snaps it to the grid (when snap to grid is on), to the sketch axes, or in line with another point (guide lines show); it never snaps onto another point or curve, so a drag does not join geometry.
- When a sketch circle that no constraint mentions is dragged by its rim, its radius follows the pointer and the center stays put; dragging its center point moves it, and a circle with a dimension or relation on it moves as a whole. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- When a linear sketch dimension's value is pulled past either extension line, its arrows move outside the extension lines pointing inward (one at each), and no line is drawn between the extension lines (only a line from the near arrow out to the value); back between the lines it is the inside double arrow again.
- In a sketch the D key (or the Dimension button, listed first in the constraint row) applies the smart dimension to the selection: one fitting constraint or dimension is applied at once; when several fit, a small menu next to the cursor lists them. One line, circle, arc or ellipse gets its length, diameter, radius or short radius; two points very near each other (the pick tolerance) get a coincident constraint, otherwise a menu of distance, horizontal and vertical distance; two parallel lines get the distance between them, other pairs of lines the angle; a point with a line or curve, two circles or arcs, and a line with a circle give the matching distance; three or more items offer only symmetric, midpoint, equal, collinear, concentric or coincident sets. With nothing usable selected a message says what to select. Never several dimensions at once. Redefinable like other hotkeys; it does nothing outside a sketch.
- A dimension that is redundant with the existing constraints is created as a reference dimension (shown in parentheses, driving nothing) with a warning. A reference dimension is made driving with the icon button in the constraint list, or by right-clicking its value in the sketch view (click a value to select it, Shift or Ctrl adds, so several switch at once); a driving dimension that becomes redundant by that turns into a reference. A redundant geometric constraint is dropped with a warning; there are no geometric reference constraints.
- A constraint or dimension the user adds beats the automatic constraints a drawing tool added: when it would be redundant with or conflict with an automatic one, that automatic constraint is removed (with a warning naming both) and the new one stays; only a clash with the user's own constraints keeps the rules above. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- The angle between two lines is the inside angle by default; dragging its value across the lines into another sector switches it to the outside angle (the supplement, never more than 180 degrees) and back.
- A regular polygon drawn with the polygon tools gets two construction circles about its center, the major one through the corners and the minor one tangent to the sides, plus equal sides, whatever the automatic constraint option says. The circles show only while the sketch is edited and never make faces or 3D geometry; dimensioning a side, or either circle, sizes the whole polygon.
- The nav cube's zoom to fit (everything or the selection) fits inside the part of the view below the top tool strip, so nothing ends up behind it. In a sketch, fitting everything also keeps the sketch's dimensions in view.
- Horizontal and vertical constraints apply to several selected lines at once; by default both kinds are applied as detected (nearly horizontal lines get horizontal, nearly vertical get vertical).
- Every dimensional constraint (any constraint holding a number) is editable on screen by double-clicking its dimension leader or its number.
- When a numeric constraint is created, its dimension appears immediately with the as-created value prefilled and ready for editing.
- Fillet and Chamfer work on a corner of two lines or arcs (click the corner point or one of its curves), and on any two lines, arcs or circles picked one after the other: the fillet is the arc of the typed radius tangent to both whose center is nearest the second click, each curve is cut or stretched to its tangent point, and tangent constraints are added; the chamfer joins the points at the typed distance along each curve (along the arc for an arc) from the corner, on the second click's side where the curves cross. A circle is cut into an arc where the fillet or chamfer meets it. Curves that do not meet are refused by Chamfer.
- An angle dimension is drawn as an arc; a point-to-line dimension is available; a dimension can be dragged by its text or its lines; long decimals on a leader stay upright. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- An arc radius leader starts at the middle of the arc, points at the arc and drags freely, with a dashed extension of the arc past an end point. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Clicking the degrees-of-freedom message in the Sketch card highlights the unconstrained geometry in yellow. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Hovering a row in the Sketch list lights that sketch's geometry in the scene, and hovering geometry lights its row; selection lights both. Double-clicking a sketch edge in the scene selects the whole sketch, and Delete removes it. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Geometry that is fully constrained is drawn in the fully-constrained color. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Over a dimension's text the cursor is the text cursor (double-click edits); over its lines it is the move cursor (drag moves it). (owner, 2026-10-27)
- The center of every circle and arc in a sketch has a small cross in the color of its curve, the same size on screen at any zoom. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Under-constrained sketch geometry is lavender (so it is not mistaken for the blue Z axis). (owner, 2026-10-27; the shade is for the owner to judge)
- The sketch origin is drawn over vertices that sit on it and wins a pick over them, so a new constraint can always reach it. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Constraint marks have no dot under them. Tangent, Fixed, Lock and Equal marks are the icon of their button; a tangent mark sits at the point where its curves touch, a little off the curve. Every mark can be clicked to select its constraint (Shift or Ctrl adds) and Delete removes it. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Right-click a Fixed mark: Dimension to the axes replaces it with the point's horizontal and vertical distances from the origin; right-click one of those distances: Fix the point turns the pair back into Fixed. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Horizontal / vertical works on a single selected line as well as on several. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- The drop list under a constraint button opens under the whole button group with the button's own text size. Every constraint button's tooltip says what it does and what to select; "does not fit the current selection" is only a warning shown when the button is clicked. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Thread hole (a Holes tool, owner request 2026-10-27): select a hole's whole round wall and it cuts a helical groove into it, the length of the wall unless a length is given; the size list gives the standard pitch, Custom uses the Pitch box, Right or Left hand.

## Layout

- The tool strip is fixed at top center. The context strip has no box behind it, fills each row from the left, spans from the bodies list to the nav cube group, wraps to two rows, and shows "... More" only when a third row would be needed.
- The Tools handle sits on the left edge of the 3D view.
- The names of the selected objects (a model selection, or in a sketch the points and curves by kind and count) are printed bottom right, between the sketch group and the measurement card, and follow the card as it grows. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- Edit cards (tool forms) sit at the right under the nav cube group like the sketch card; the measurement card moves left only when an edit card reaches it.
- The measurement card starts collapsed and sits bottom right. 
- Dark theme only for now.
- When faces, edges or vertices are selected and the view is right-clicked without dragging, a menu offers "Select parent bodies" (the owning bodies replace the selection); with a single face or edge, the Select similar choices come first. (Provisional.)
- A resize grip (cube) sits on its anchor with its stem running inward toward the solid; only the cube is grabbable. A move arrow is grabbed only by its arrowhead, not its line.
- A tip stays on screen until its Got it is clicked, even after the action that raised it ends. The chips (such as Add Parallel Face) and the hint line never overlap: the hint line and tip stand above the chips. There is no separate sketch key hint line.
- The sketch plane move and turn gizmos are off by default; the sketch card button shows them.
- Sketch constraint tools that only apply to edges ignore selected vertices instead of doing nothing.
- While a sketch is open the top strip has a second row of constraint buttons, each an icon with its name, the Dimension (smart dimension) button first. Buttons whose constraints the smart dimension reaches through every selection are not in the row (length and distances, short radius, point to line distance, symmetric); the audit is in archive/smart-dimension-plan.md. Horizontal distance and Vertical distance are a drop list under the Dimension button, and the strict Horizontal and Vertical constraints a drop list under the combined Horizontal / vertical button (a small arrow beside each); a plain click on the button itself keeps the smart behavior, and the D, H and other hotkeys work as before. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- Dimension values are always drawn upright (horizontal on the screen), whatever direction their line runs, including vertical dimensions (owner, 2026-10-24).
- The sketch's horizontal and vertical axes can be picked like lines (when no other curve or point is near) and dimensioned or constrained to; the Symmetric button is always on the strip and takes two points about a line, three points about the third, or two lines about a line or a point.
- A Symmetric constraint shows a "Sym" mark at each of its mirrored points (not at the axis or line), and mirrors the points about the chosen axis or line.
- A Fixed point stays where it is when dragged; a circle is drawn as fully constrained only when its radius is fixed as well.
- In a sketch the top bar's Undo and Redo and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y step through the sketch's own edits; a constraint that is dropped says which constraints it repeats or conflicts with.
- Shift or Ctrl+click on a kept sketch's tinted region adds it to (or takes it out of) the picked regions; all picked regions of one sketch are selected as faces together.
- Hovering a sketch in the sketches list lights its row and its curves in the view (white); a picked sketch is orange in both. Hovering a sketch's curves or tinted region in the view lights it the same way. Double-clicking a sketch row opens it for editing; renaming and deleting are in its right-click menu.
- Double-clicking a sketch's curves or tinted region in the 3D view selects the whole sketch (the Delete key then removes it).
- A visible sketch region drawn over a coplanar face of the model comes first: hovering it does not light the face, and pressing it picks the region instead of starting a drag of the face (unless that face is already selected).
- Picked sketch regions are faces with the sketch's own normal, whichever way a body face under them points. A selection of body faces together with picked sketch regions can be pushed or pulled: the body faces move first, then the regions are extruded into the result. (owner, 2026-10-29)
- While numbers are typed for a shape being drawn, the shape is previewed at the typed sizes and each size is drawn as a real dimension (extension lines and dimension line for a rectangle's width and height, a line's or slot's length and width; a radius leader for a circle, polygon or ellipse; an angle arc for an arc's sweep) holding its value; the number being typed is orange (no caret).
- A circle's default dimension (typed while drawing, and its live size) is its diameter; an arc's is its radius; polygons keep the radius. Right-clicking a radius or diameter value offers Show as diameter or Show as radius (the size is kept). Two circles or arcs selected offer the distance between the centers and, when they do not overlap, the gap between the rims.
- While a rectangle, circle or polygon is drawn (before anything is typed) its current sizes are drawn as real dimension leaders in the construction gray with the value in parentheses, like reference dimensions; reference dimensions are gray everywhere. (owner, 2026-10-27)
- A dimension value clicked in the sketch view is selected (orange; Shift or Ctrl adds) and the Delete key removes it, undoable; the geometry stays.
- The smart dimension on two separated points is the length between them (no menu); horizontal distance and vertical distance are in the drop list under the Dimension button.
- In a sketch, Enter with no drawing tool active (the Select tool, nothing being typed) starts the drawing tool used last again; Enter while a line chain is open still ends the chain (owner, 2026-10-24).
- Concentric and nested circles or arcs selected together offer the gap between their rims as a dimension; clicking a dimension's leader or extension line selects it like clicking its value.
- Trim takes two clicks on a line (owner, 2026-10-25): the first cuts the line at every crossing with other lines and circles and turns the piece under the click into construction geometry; a second click on that piece deletes it. Changing tool or stepping undo or redo forgets the marked pieces. The cutting is the Split tool's, so direction constraints stay on the pieces.
- Split adds an automatic Collinear constraint to a line with no direction constraint; two lines that share an end point are collinear by making them parallel, with no conflict reported.
- Split on a point that lies on a line (a point-on-line constraint or touching it) cuts the line exactly there; the point becomes the end of both pieces, so it joins them (shown as an automatic Coincident row). (owner request, 2026-10-26)
- Split on an ellipse cuts it open at the click into one elliptical arc that starts and ends there; Split on an elliptical arc cuts it into two arcs that share the cut point and the ellipse's axis end points (the first keeps the arc), and Split on a point that lies on either does the same at that point. A click near an end of an arc, or an old ellipse without axis ends, is refused with a message. Trim does not yet cut ellipses or elliptical arcs. (provisional, ellarc agent, 2026-10-26)
- Arc by center (center, start, end): the arc runs the way the pointer moves from the start, clockwise or counterclockwise by the short way round (up to 180 degrees); the preview and the committed arc agree. A typed sweep in degrees is always counterclockwise. (owner, 2026-10-25)
- Arc by 3 points takes its clicks in the order start point, end point, then the point the arc passes through; after the second click the preview is the arc through the pointer, and the chord alone when the three points are in a line. (owner, 2026-10-25; replaces start, through point, end)
- In a sketch, when a constraint or dimension is applied from the strip, D or the smart dimension menu, the geometry selection is cleared; one that is dropped as conflicting leaves the selection as it was. (owner, 2026-10-25)
- Construction geometry in a sketch (toggled construction curves, polygon circles, projected edges, trimmed pieces) is drawn dashed in gray, with dashes of constant screen size, and always beneath the normal curves; the sketch points still draw over everything. A selected construction curve is drawn orange and solid. (owner, 2026-10-25)
- A new sketch ellipse gets two construction lines, one joining the opposite ends of the major axis and one the minor axis; they share the ellipse's end points, so they follow it when it is edited, and selecting one alone offers its length as a dimension. They are not part of the profile. Deleting the ellipse leaves them. (owner, 2026-10-25)
- The Ellipse arc tool (next to Ellipse in the strip) takes five clicks: the center, the end of the long axis, a point for the short radius, then the start and the end of the arc; start and end are taken to the ellipse by angle, and the arc runs the way the pointer moves from the start. It is an ellipse with start and end points, so it has the same axis ends and construction lines, Major width and Minor width dimensions, and the two ends stay on the ellipse; a start clicked at an axis end is that point. Typed: major width, minor width and a sweep in degrees (the arc then starts at the long axis end and sweeps counterclockwise). A closed outline of arcs and lines is a profile. (provisional, ellarc agent, 2026-10-26)
- A rectangle drawn by its center also gets that center as a point, held halfway between two opposite corners (Symmetric); if the center was clicked on a point it is that point, and if on a line, a sketch axis or a line midpoint it is also made coincident with it (Point on or Midpoint). These constraints are always made, whatever the automatic-constraints setting. (owner, 2026-10-25)
- Diameter dimension values start with the capital Ø (full capital height, like the R of a radius), in sketches, the typed-size dimensions and the 3D measurements.
- Selecting a body shows its volume (mm³) and its mass (g) in the measurement card; the mass uses the density of the body's own material, or of the Default material setting (PLA by default) when it has none. Several bodies also show totals.
- Right-click a body in the body list > Material: opens a list (Default, then every material); the choice belongs to that body only, is saved with the project and is one undo step. A duplicate keeps its material. The 3D view still draws every body with the Default material's look.
- Resize chamfer has two distances, each with its own gizmo on the neighbor face it is measured along; dragging or typing one changes only that distance (both 0 leaves the sharp edge, one alone at 0 is refused).
- Draft pivots each face about the plane through its own centroid by default; the form's Axis list also offers Picked point (the Pick axis button asks for a click on any edge of the model, its own face's or another, and uses the edge's middle point; the neutral plane is square to the pull direction, so it contains the edge when the edge is square to the pull) and Model base (the lowest point, the earlier default, still used by editing a wall's angle tag).
- When Pliable runs in the desktop window, the window title shows the version ("Pliable 0.4.0 (alpha)"); the version is one number kept equal in the core, the shell and its settings (a core test checks it). Copy diagnostics also lists the run mode, machine, graphics renderer, screen, core uptime and open body count.
- The degrees of freedom message lights the curves and points that still have freedom (including a circle whose radius is free) in yellow; each separate shape is checked alone so large sketches are not left half checked.
- Cut with plane: the plane comes from Pick plane (a flat face), a datum plane, an origin plane or a kept sketch button; its arrow and two rings move and tilt it; the Remove side list reads None, Side 1 and Side 2, and Side 1 and Side 2 are written in the color of the body on that side of the plane.
- Cut with plane (and the other plane forms): Plane: 3 points (click three corners), Plane: edge + point (click a straight edge, then a corner) and Plane: face offset/angle (type the offset and angle in the two boxes beside it, then click a face) fill the plane from the picked geometry; the arrow and rings then move and tilt it as before.
- Revolve face: its angle has the same pie-slice grip as Circular array's, whatever way the axis was chosen (a selected edge or global axis, or the Pick axis and X, Y, Z axis buttons); with no axis chosen yet there is no grip.
- The Fillet/Chamfer form has a Two distances button: it reopens as Chamfer (two distances) on the first face beside the first selected edge and the selected edges, with the typed distance as both distances. No key is bound to it, so typed-number Tab and Alt+click face cycling are unchanged.
- While Fillet/Chamfer is armed with edges selected, a Two distances chip appears with the other chips; it switches to Chamfer (two distances) on the first face beside the first edge. In that tool an edge that does not border the reference face is measured from its own first neighboring face.
- Pressing M when a push/pull drag has no straight edge to measure to, or when no face is being dragged, says why in the status line; pressing M just after the press, before the pointer has moved, starts measure mode. A drag whose button went up unseen (measure mode left with Escape after release) ends at the next pointer move, so the measurement card and keys keep working.
- Holding Shift with something selected shows a hint line that Shift+click adds to the selection and that a tool or chip comes next.
- The Tangent arc tool (next to Arc by 3 points) takes two clicks: the end of a line or arc (or the line or arc itself, which takes its nearer end), then where the new arc ends; the arc leaves the curve in the direction it was heading, and a tangent constraint is always added, whatever the automatic-constraints option says. An end straight ahead of the curve is refused (a line fits, not an arc). Ellipses, circles and splines cannot start one.
- Arc by ends and radius (next to Arc by 3 points): the radius is typed in the sketch card, then the start, the end and a third click choose among the four arcs of that radius (two centers, near or far side) the one nearest the third click; a radius below half the distance between the ends is refused. The radius is kept as a dimension. The center Arc's card has Start angle (degrees counterclockwise from the horizontal axis) and Sweep (counterclockwise, above 0 and below 360); blank leaves that choice to the pointer, a number places the start direction, or the end at that sweep, whatever the pointer does.
- Circle tangent to curves: pick lines, arcs or circles one after another; the third pick makes the circle touching all three (of the up to eight such circles, the one whose center is nearest that click), and after two picks a click on empty space makes the circle of the card's radius touching both, nearest that click (the radius is kept as a dimension). Tangent constraints are added. Arc tangent to curves takes two picks and then a click anywhere that chooses the side; the arc runs the short way between the two touching points, its ends are held on the curves, tangent to both, with the card's radius as a dimension. Neither trims the curves (Fillet does).
- The Round corner and Bevel corner buttons (a selected corner point, size from the box beside them) work on any corner of exactly two lines or arcs, like Fillet and Chamfer; with no pointer, the fillet nearest the corner itself is made.
- Trim and Extend also find the crossings of a line with ellipses, elliptical arcs (only where the arc is) and splines, as they do with lines, circles and arcs (owner row, decided by the hierarchy). Trim still cuts and removes lines only.
- The degrees of freedom number in the sketch card includes the short radius of an older ellipse that has no axis end points (one degree each, until a minor radius dimension fixes it), so the number agrees with the yellow highlight.
- Shift or Ctrl+click right on a kept sketch's curve chooses the whole sketch beside the face and edge selection (its curves turn orange; click the curve again to let it go; a click on empty space or any committed step lets it go too). Point picks in the view (Move/Rotate and arrays: base and destination point) snap to the ends, centers and line middles of visible kept sketches, and to a point on their curves.
- With a kept sketch chosen (Shift or Ctrl+click on its curve), Array along path and Sweep face along edge accept it as the path in place of selected edges (Sweep still needs its one face; Array along path then needs nothing else). The sketch's drawn curves, construction lines left out, must join into one chain. Choosing the sketch makes the tools appear in the strip; applying one lets the choice go.
- The sketch bar's View button (after XY, XZ and YZ) starts a sketch on the plane square to the camera, through the point the view turns about, with the screen's right as the sketch's x axis; the plane stays fixed when the view is turned afterwards.
- The circular array's angle is a pie slice about the axis: a short line from the source toward the axis, an arc round to the last copy with the degrees on it, and a short line from there toward the axis; the grip rides the arc and follows the pointer round the axis, the degrees can be typed mid-drag, in the card, or by double-clicking the value on the arc, and the angle is at most 360. The count is a whole number from 1 (default 2) shown over the source object; the mouse wheel over it changes it by one and double-clicking it types it. (owner, 2026-10-26)
- Counterbore and countersink sizes can be entered either as a diameter or as an offset from the hole (the card's size-by list picks which field is used; the diameter is the hole's plus twice the offset); the countersink has an angle gizmo (an arc on the cone's side, turning about the mouth) and an Angles list of the five standard head angles (82, 90, 100, 110, 120). (owner, 2026-10-26)
- Selecting a hole (one face of its bore, its shoulder or cone, its rim circle, its center dot, or any part of a split wall) opens the Hole Editor card in the push/pull card's place; several holes selected by their walls open it too. A boss, a flat face alone, or a mixed selection opens nothing. The card closes when the selection changes or on a click on empty space, applying nothing, and Escape closes it; the hole's edit arrows and center-dot drag work as before. (owner, 2026-10-29; the card's first version, provisional until hand-checked)
- The tool drawer and the command search list one Hole Editor entry in place of Add hole, Add counterbore, Add countersink, Add counterbore to hole, Add countersink to hole, Edit hole, Thread hole, Align holes and Distribute holes; the Resize, Move, Fill and Pattern buttons stay for now. With a flat face selected it starts a new hole (Simple, Counterbore, Countersink); with a hole selected it edits it; with nothing fitting it waits for a selection. (owner, 2026-10-29)
